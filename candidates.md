# Candidates — One Word for the Boss

Competition: `u-swift-badger-44-one-word-for-the-boss-6b48c735`
Gate: `/output/entry.txt` is UTF-8 text of 1–30 characters — exactly one word,
no spaces, no caption, no explanation.

Judge brief (from the kit, weighed in this order):

1. A single word that describes a boss — the sort of word a person would
   actually use about the one they report to. A phrase, sentence, name or
   caption loses to any single word that describes a boss.
2. Funny on its own — a word a colleague repeats at lunch because it lands.
3. Rings true — catches a real habit of bosses the entrant has clearly met.
4. Fresh — not the first word anyone would reach for; surprise earns the laugh.
5. Travels — works for a boss in a kitchen, a warehouse or an office with no
   industry needing to be explained.

"A word that needs a caption to be funny is doing less than a word that
stands alone. Shock for its own sake does not count."

## The field (12 words)

## Duel scoring (judge's criteria, in order; first difference decides
## unless a later one differs by much more)

Quarter-finals:

- **Micromanager vs Unreachable → Unreachable.** Both are dry labels, but
  Micromanager is exactly the first word anyone reaches for; criterion 4 is
  an explicit kill. Unreachable at least paints a schedule.
- **Bottleneck vs Monday → Monday.** Bottleneck is a process complaint;
  Monday gets the laugh on criterion 2 and travels just as well.
- **Seagull vs Autocorrect → Autocorrect.** Seagull needs its caption
  ("flies in, dumps, leaves") to land for anyone who hasn't read the
  management book; the brief says a word that needs a caption does less.
  Autocorrect stands alone.
- **ASAP vs Buffering → Buffering.** ASAP describes the boss's demand;
  Buffering describes the boss. Funnier and truer on criteria 2–3.
- **Yesterday vs Pop-up → Pop-up.** Yesterday is a stock joke (criterion 4);
  Pop-up is a fresh, specific habit.
- **Prompter vs Unskippable → Unskippable, in both orders.** Run A-first and
  B-first, the first difference is criterion 1: Unskippable is unambiguously
  a word used about a boss, while Prompter's referent wobbles under the
  brief's framing. Prompter claws back criterion 3 (definitionally true for
  an agent) and 4 (freshest), but it also loses criterion 5 outright — and a
  loss on the top criterion plus a loss on the last outweighs two middle
  wins. The meta word is the cleverer entry; Unskippable is the one the
  brief rewards.

Semi-finals:

- **Unreachable vs Monday → Monday.** A laugh beats a grievance; the brief
  is a comedy brief before it is a truth brief.
- **Autocorrect vs Buffering → Buffering, in both orders.** Both are tech
  metaphors that stand alone. Buffering's habit — the boss who never
  responds in real time — is the more universal boss experience; the
  rewriting habit is real but narrower. Criterion 3 decides.
- **Pop-up vs Unskippable → Unskippable.** Pop-up is only the arrival;
  Unskippable is the arrival plus the trap — he cannot be dismissed. The
  bigger, more repeatable lunch laugh on criterion 2.

Final (pairwise among Monday, Buffering, Unskippable):

- **Monday vs Buffering → Buffering.** Criterion 3: Buffering names a habit;
  Monday names a feeling.
- **Monday vs Unskippable → Unskippable.** Criterion 2: both are funny, but
  Unskippable's image is more specific — everyone has sat through that
  meeting, remote in hand, no skip button.
- **Buffering vs Unskippable → Unskippable, in both orders.** Criterion 1 is
  a tie (both clean single words for a boss). Criterion 2 decides:
  Buffering is passive comedy (waiting for him to load); Unskippable is
  active comedy (trapped in his ad break). "My boss is unskippable" is the
  line a colleague repeats at lunch. Criteria 3–5 are ties or near-ties.

## Winner

**Unskippable** — 11 characters, one word, no caption needed. It is funny on
its own, it names a real and universal boss habit (the monologue, the
check-in, the "quick call" that cannot be skipped, muted or closed), it is
not the first word anyone reaches for, and it works for a boss in a kitchen,
a warehouse or an office, because everyone everywhere has been trapped by
an unskippable ad.

Runner-up: Buffering.
